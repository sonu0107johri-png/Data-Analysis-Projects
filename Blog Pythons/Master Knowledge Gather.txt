RSS History

1. Established - 27-09-1925 - Vijaydashmi 
   Founder - Dr. Keshav Baliram Hedgewar
   Headquarter - Nagpur, Maharashtra
   Memberships - 50-60 lakhs, 56859 branches
   Official Language - Sanskrit, Hindi
   Sarkaryavah (General Secretary) - Dattatreya Hosabale
   Sarsanghchalak (chief) - Mohan Bhagwat
2. An Indian right-wing Hindutva volunteer paramilitary organization.
3. Parent and leader of a large group of Hindutva organizations called the Sangh Parivar which includes Bhartiya Janta Party - the current ruling party of India under the leadership of Prime Minister Narendra Modi.
4. Build on 27th September 1925, the organization's initial objective was to build character and instill "Hindu discipline" in order to unite the Hindu community and establish a Hindu nation.
	- The organization aims to empower the Hindu community and preserve Indian culture and its civilizational values through the propagation of Hindutva ideology.
	- The organization has also been described as an organization based on the principle of Hindu Supremacism. It has also been accused of intolerance towards minorities, especially Muslims.
	- After independence, it developed into an influential Hindu nationalist organization, establishing several affiliated organizations through which it operated schools, charities, and clubs.
5. it was banned for four days in 1947, and subsequently banned three more times:
	- in 1948 when Nathuram Godse assassinated Mahatma Gandhi
	- between 1975 - 1977 during the Emergency along with Jana Sangh
	- in 1992 after the demolition of the Babri Masjid.   
6. After the Emergency was lifted, the Jana Sangh merged with the Janata Party, and a coalition government was formed at the Centre under the Prime Ministership of Morarji Desai. After the Emergency, the political importance of this organization gradually increased, culminating in the formation of a political party like BJP, which is generally seen as the political wing of the RSS.

	- Dr. Keshavrao Baliram Hedgewar - Doctorji - 1925 - 1940
	- Madhav Sadashivrao Golwalkar - Guruji - 1940 - 1973

8. The organization completed its 100th year on Vijaydashmi, 02-10-2025.
	- Prime Minister Narendra Modi released a special postage stamp and a ₹100 commemorative coin to mark the occasion.
	- For the first time in the history of Indian currency, the coin features the image of Bharat Mata.
	- Former President Ram Nath Kovind was the chief guest at the RSS centenary celebration and the celebration of the auspicious festival of Vijaydashmi and October 2 (Birthday of Mahatma Gandhi).
	- The event was held at the Reshimbag grounds in Nagpur, where the RSS was founded in 1925.
9. Sarsanghchalak holds the top position who gives direction to the entire Sangh. 
	- Each Sarsanghchalak announces his successor.
10. Most of the Sangh's work is carried out through the Shakha (branch), where volunteers meet for an hour in public places in the morning or evening.
	- Common Shakha activities include sports, yoga including Suryanamaskar, parade worship, and intellectual discussions on cultural aspects of India and the world.
	- Types of Sangh:
		- Prabhat Shakha - morning 
		- Evening Shakha
		- Night Shakha
		- Milan - Meeting
		- Sangh-Mandali
	- Apart from the 56859 branches in India, branches operate in other countries as well, but this work is not carried out under the RSS banner.
		- in some places, it runs through the 'Bhartiya Swayamsevak Sangh'.
		- And in other, it runs through the 'Hindu Swayamsevak Sangh'
	- the position of 'Karyavah' is the highest in a branch.
		- After that, there is the position of 'Chief Teacher' to ensure the smooth running of the daily operations of the branches.
 	- Any member who comes to the branch voluntarily is called a 'Swayamsevak'
11. The first celebration of the RSS is Varsha Pratipada.
	- Also called - Nav Samvatsara
	- It is on this day that Lord Brahma created the universe and all the gods assumed responsibility for its management.
	- marks the beginning of the New Year in the Indian or Hindu tradition.
	- Also celebrated as Chaitra Shukla Pratipada.
12. The second festival is Guru Purnima.
	- It is celebrated with great devotion throughout the country. In India, the Guru is considered greater than God.
	- In Sangh, the God is considered not a person but the saffron flag. The saffron flag is a symbol of India's eternal culture. India's entire sage tradition is hidden within this symbol.
	- This is why, through this festival, volunteers connect with the invaluable heritage of the country's ancient culture and take pride in Indian values.
	- Guru Purnima is also celebrated in the Sangh as Guru Dakshina.
13. The third festival of the Sangh is Raksha Bandhan. 
	- Today, this festival is associated with the love and protection of brothers and sisters in society.
	- In the RSS, this festival is celebrated in a different way. On this day, all the volunteers gather at the branch and tie the thread of protection to each other. In this way, they strengthen the tradition of helping each other for their brothers, their religion, and their culture. This festival instills in everyone the culture of collective protection.


Dyatlov Pass Incident

1. The Dyatlov Pass Incident refers to the mysterious deaths of nine experienced Soviet ski hikers in the northern Ural Mountains on the night of February 1, 1959

2. The expedition was led by 23-year old Igor Dyatlov, a group of eight students and graduates plus one tour guide set out for Mount Otorten.  
	a. They pitched their tent on the slope of Kholat Syakhi (Dead Mountain), an area known to the local Mansi people.
	b. In sub-zero temperatures, the hikers abruptly cut their way out of their tent from the inside and fled without shoes or proper winter gear.

3. Search parties found the tent sliced open and partially collapsed. 
	a. The first five bodies were found near a cedar tree and scattered between the tree line and the tent.
	b. The remaining four bodies were discovered months later in a snow-filled ravine deeper in the forest.

4. While some hikers died of hypothermia, three sustained horrific, severe, physical trauma, including major skull fractures and crushed chests comparable to the force of a severe car crash. 
	a. Some bodies also had missing soft tissues like eyes or tongues.

5. The Official Conclusion was that the deaths were due to an unknown compelling force that led to the case files being classified.
	a. Recent scientific re-evaluations lean toward a rare, slow-moving slab avalanche triggered by katabatic winds and unstable snow accumulation from their campsite excavation, which forced a panicked evacuation followed by hypothermia and severe crushing injuries in the ravine.
	b. Decades of speculation have blamed military rocket tests, katabatic wind panic, or local Mansi tribes, though these lack supporting evidence. 
 
6. The nine hikers who tragically perished in the Dyatlov Pass Incident were primarily students and alumni of the Ural Polytechnic Institute (UPI) in Sverdlovsk (now Yekaterinburg)
	a. They were highly capable, Grade II certified ski hikers who embarked on this extreme expedition to earn their Grade III certification - the highest rank available in the Soviet Union at the time.
	b. Igor Alekseievich Dyatlov - M - Age-23 - fifth-year radio engineering student and an experienced cross-country athlete - he designed much of the group's custom equipment, including a portable wood stove they used in their tents
	c. Zinaida Alekseevna Kolmogorova - F - Age-22 - fourth-year radio engineering student and known for her infectious high energy, resilience, and popularity within the university's hiking club - she had previously survived a toxic viper bite during a different expedition. 
	d. Lyudmila Alexandrovna Dubinina - F - Age-20 - economics student and deeply passionate communist youth organizer; an experienced outdoorswoman - she was the youngest member of the group who also served as the expedition's unofficial treasurer and photographer.
	e. Yuri Nikolaievich Doroshenko - M - Age-21 - economics student specializing in power industries; he was remarkably tall and strong, once famously fending off a wild bear with a geological hammer during a previous trek - romantically involved with Zinaida 
	f. Yuri (Georgiy) Alexeievich Krivonischenko - M - Age-23 - a recent construction engineering graduate who worked at a secret nuclear facility in Chelyabinsk-40 (the site of the infamous 1957 Kyshtym disaster) - he was the group's entertainer, known for singing, playing the mandolin, and being the life of the party.   
	g. Rustem Vladimirovich Slobodin - M - Age-23 - recent mechanical engineering graduate and an athletic. quiet long-distance runner. His father was a university professor.
	h. Alexander Sergelevich Kolevatov - M - Age-24 - a nuclear physics student who had previously moved to Moscow to work at a classified research institute before returning to Sverdlovsk - considered as methodical. strictly organized, and analytical.
	i. Nicolai Vladimirovich Thibeaux-Brignolles - M - Age-23 - a recent civil engineering graduate. Came from a prominent family of French ancestry, his father had been a prominent engineer imprisoned in a Soviet Gulag during Stalin's purges - known for his sharp with and artistic sketching.
	j. Semyon (Alexander) Alexandrovich Zolotaryov - M - Age-38 - A decorated World War II combat veteran who survived the front lines against Nazi Germany - Guide of the group - He joined the group to gain his master of sports certification - because of his age, military tattoos, and unknown background, he is central to many conspiracy theories.
	k. Yuri Yefimovich Yudin - M - Age-21 - an economics student who set out with the group on January 23. However, on January 28, a severe flare-up of chronic rheumatism and joint pain forced him to say goodbye and turn back to the village of Vizhay. This medical misfortune ultimately saved his life. - Lone Survivor

7. The 1959 Soviet criminal case files and official autopsy reports led by the forensic expert Dr. Boris Vozrozhdenny provide a stark, graphic record of the scene and the victim's bodies. The forensic findings were divided into two distinct phases based on when the bodies were discovered.
	Phase I - The campsite and the first five bodies - February - March 1959
		a. Tent 
			a.1. partially collapsed and covered in snow.
			a.2. slash marks from the inside with multiple knife cuts.
			a.3. all heavy winter clothes, boots, and gear were left neatly inside
		b. Tracks
			b.1. Eight or nine sets of footprints walked down the slope in a relatively orderly, unpanicked manner
				b.1.a. The hikers were either completely barefoot, wearing only socks, or wearing a single shoe. 
		c. Cedar Tree Site
			c.1. Roughly 1.5km downstream at the forest edge, rescuers found the frozen remains of a makeshift campfire under a massive Siberian cedar tree.
			c.2. Beneath it lay the first two bodies - Yuri Krivonischenko and Yuri Doroshenko - dressed only in their underwear and socks.
				c.2.a. Both died strictly of hypothermia.
				c.2.b. Krivonischenko's hands was deeply charred - forensic evidence showed he had bitten off a piece of skin from his own hand. This is a known symptom of severe frostbite and nerve death, where a person cannot feel pain and act erratically.
				c.2.c. Branches on cedar tree were snapped up to 5m high
				c.2.d. Skin tissue was found embedded in the bark, indicating they frantically tried to climb it to see the tent.
		d. The Line of return
			d.1. Between the tree and the tent, three more bodies were found scattered along the slope.
				d.1.a. Igor Dyatlov - 300m from the tree 
				d.1.b. Zinaida Kolmogorova - 480m from the tree
				d.1.c. Rustem Slobodin - 630m from the tree.
				d.1.d. Their body positions indicated they were crawling or fighting their way back towards the tent when they died.
				d.1.e. All three died of hypothermia
				d.1.f. They had minor abrasions and skin loss on their faces and hands from crawling over ice. 
				d.1.g. Slobodin's autopsy revealed a 17cm internal fracture in his skull. Dr. Vozrozhdenny noted that this injury was non-fatal and that Slobodin was still conscious and moving after sustaining it. 
	Phase 2: The Ravine and Massive trauma - May 1959
		a. More than two months later, as the snow melted, the remaining four hikers were found buried under 4m of snow in a ravine further down the forest line. These bodies wore more clothes than the first five, having stripped items from their dead companions. 
		b. Thibeaux-Brignolles - Massive Craniocerebral trauma - depressed skull fracture on the right side of his head with multiple bone fragments forced into the brain tissue.
		c. Dubinina - Internal Hemorrhaging / Chest Crushing - Multiple bilateral rib features - 10 broken ribs
		d. Zolotaryov - Internal Hemorrhaging / Chest Crushing - Multiple flat chest features - 5 broken ribs
		e. Kolevatov - Hypothermia - No fatal bone trauma, died of exposure alongside the others.

Project Azorean

1. Project Azorian, also known as Jennifer by the press after its Top Secret Security Compartment, was a highly classified, ultra-complex US Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) operation carried out in 1974 to covertly raise a sunken Soviet ballistic missile submarine, K-129, from the bottom of the Pacific Ocean, using purpose built ship - Hughes Glomar Explorer.

2. Orchestrated at the height of the Cold War, it is widely considered one of the most audacious, technologically ambitious, and expensive intelligence coups in American history, costing roughly $800 million at the time (equivalent to nearly $4 billion today).

3. In March 1968, The Soviet Golf-II class submarine K-129 suffered a catastrophic onboard accident and sank with all hands roughly 1560 miles northwest of Hawaii.
		1.a. On February 24, 1968, K-129, a Soviet Project 629A ballistic missile submarine attached to the 15th Submarine Squadron of the Soviet Pacific Fleet, left Rybachiy Naval Base in Kamchatka on a routine missile patrol - the boat's third since completing a major modernization the previous year.
		1.b. On the first day, the sub cruised out to deep water, conducted a test dive, surfaced to radio in, and embarked for its patrol station.
		1.c. The sub was to make standard radio contact with its commanders in Kamchatka when crossing the 180th meridian and when arriving on station. But K-129 missed its designated check-ins and did not respond to communication attempts. 
		1.d. By the third week of March, the submarine was declared missing.
	a. While the Soviet Navy launched a massive, unsuccessful search, they could not pinpoint the wreckage.
		1.a. In April 1968, many Soviet Pacific Fleet surface and air assets deployed to the North Pacific Ocean were observed performing unusual search operations.
			1.a.1. The activity was evaluated by the United States Office of Naval Intelligence (ONI) as a possible reaction to the loss of a Soviet submarine.
		1.b. Soviet surface ship searches were centered on a location known to be associated with Soviet Golf II-class strategic ballistic missile (SSB) diesel submarine patrol routes.
			1.b.1. These submarines carried three nuclear missiles in an extended sail/conning tower and were routinely deployed within missile range of the US West Coast.
		1.c. After weeks of searching, the Soviets were unable to locate the sunken boat, and the Soviet Pacific Fleet operations gradually returned to normal. 
	b. However, using a sophisticated web of acoustic data from the US Navy's underwater surveillance technology, American intelligence successfully located the submarine resting 16500 feet (over 3 miles) below the ocean surface.
		1.a. US used data recorded by four Air Force Technical Applications Centre (AFTAC) sites and the Adak Sound Surveillance System (SOSUS) array.
			1.a.1. The US Navy analyzed acoustic data recorded by the SOSUS hydrophone network in the Northern Pacific - four AFTAC sites and the Adak, Alaska SOSUS array - and found evidence of the implosion that had sunk the Russian sub.
			1.a.2 Naval Facility (NAVFAC) Point Sur, south of Monterey, California, isolated a sonic signature on its low-frequency array recordings of an implosion that had occurred on March 8 1965.
				1.a.1.a. Using NAVFAC Point Sur's date and time of the event, NAVFAC Adak and the US West Coast NAVFAC were also able to isolate the acoustic event.
			1.a.3. With five SOSUS lines-of-bearing, Naval Intelligence was able to localize the site of the K-129 wreck to the vicinity of 40.1 degree N latitude and 179.9 degree E longitude, close to the International Date Line.
		1.b. The US identified an acoustic event on March 8 that likely originated from an explosion aboard the submarine, and was able to determine the location within five nautical miles - 5.8 miles.
		1.c. The submarine USS Halibut located the boat using the Fish - a towed 12-foot, two-short-ton collection of cameras, strobe lights, and sonar that was built to withstand extreme depths.
	c. The target was a gold mine of intelligence. It held:
		1.a. Nuclear Torpedoes
		1.b. Ballistic Missiles
		1.c. Highly Coveted Cryptological Codebooks
	d. The reasons that this project was undertaken included the recovery of an intact R-21 nuclear missile and cryptological documents and equipment.

4. Lifting a multi-thousand-ton submarine from three miles down had never been attempted and required entirely new engineering disciplines. To keep the operation entirely covert, the CIA engineered an elaborate front.
	a. The CIA teamed up with eccentric billionaire Howard Hughes, who publicly announced a venture to mine valuable manganese nodules from the deep ocean floor.
	b. A massive, custom-built salvage vessel named the Hughes Glomar Explorer was constructed.
		1.a. The US designed the recovery ship and its lifting cradle using concepts developed with Global Marine that used their precision stability equipment to keep the ship nearly stationary above the target while lowering nearly three miles of pipe.
			1.a.1. They worked with the scientists to develop methods for preserving paper that had been underwater for years in hopes of being able to recover and read the submarine's codebooks.
		1.b. It featured a hidden interior bay (the 'Moon Pool') with bottom-opening doors so the submarine could be pulled inside away from the view of passing Soviet ships or satellites.
		1.c. A massive mechanical capture vehicle nicknamed 'Clementine' was built to wrap around the hull of the submarine.

5. In July 1974, the Glomar Explorer arrived over the wreck site.
	a. Operating under intense pressure and closely watched for weeks by a suspicious Soviet ocean tug, engineers successfully lowered Clementine via miles of heavy steel pipe and grabbed the submarine.
	b. As winches slowly pulled the submarine up, a critical structural failure occurred. A section of the claw fractured, causing roughly two-thirds of the K-129 hull to break apart and plunge back down to the abyssal plain.

6. Despite the partial collapse, the mission was considered an engineering miracle and an intelligence success.
	a. The recovered one-third section contained two nuclear torpedoes, various operational machinery, and the bodies of six Soviet sailors.
	b. The US military recovered the bodies and later gave them a formal, respectful burial at sea with full military honors, a ceremony filmed and eventually handed over to Russia after the Cold War ended.

7. Plans for a second recovery mission to gather the remaining wreckage were permanently halted in early 1975 when journalists exposed the secret project.
	a. When inundated with press inquiries about the incident, the CIA formulated a unique phrase to protect national security.
	 1.a. This legal defense - stating that the agency could "neither confirm nor deny" the existence of the information - became known worldwide as the 'Glomar response'
