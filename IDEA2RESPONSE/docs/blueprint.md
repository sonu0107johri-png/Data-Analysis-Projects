#IDEA2RESPONSE

## Product Direction
Career Autopilot Platform

---

## Core Concept
The platform analyses evidence from a user's work, projects, skills, certifications, interests, and behaviour to suggest a suitable long-term career direction and track progress

---

## Evidence Weight (Version 1)

Profile Evidence -> 15%

Skill Evidence -> 20%

Project Evidence -> 25%

Device Evidence -> 0%

Physical World Evidence -> 0%

Career Preference Evidence -> 25%

## Career Score Formula (Version 1)

Career Score = (Profile Score * 0.15 + Skill Score * 0.2 + Project Score * 0.25 + Behaviour Score * 0.15 + Career Preference Score * 0.25)

Output:

0-30 -> Exploration Stage

31-50 -> Emerging Direction

51-75 -> Strong Alignment

76-90 -> High Career Match

91-100 -> Exceptional Career Fit

#### Interview Philosophy

The interview is not a traditional questionnaire.

Its purpose is to prgogressively discover the user through conversation, situational choices, behavioural scenarios, preferences, reactions,
trade-offs, reasoning tasks, and follow-up questions.

The system should avoid relying on direct self-description whenever behavioural or indirect evidence can provide a stronger signal.

No single answer should define a trait.

Every answer may provide evidence for multiple traits, and every important trait should ideally be supported by multiple independent observations.

Questions are selected dynamically according to:
1. Traits that have not yet been explored.
2. Traits with insufficient evidence.
3. Contradictory evidence.
4. Newly discovered behavioural patterns.
5. Career hypotheses requiring validation.
6. Previous answers that require deeper investigation.
7. Confidence levels of existing conclusions

The interview therefore has no permanently fixed sequence.

The Human Discovery Engine continously decides what information would be most valuable to discover next.

#### Interview Structure

The interview follows adaptive phases rather than a fixed questionnaire.

Phase-I - Orientation:
Establish basic non-evaluative information required to understand the user's current situation and provide context for later questions

Phase-II - Broad Discovery:
Explore many different areas of the Human Profile using varied scenarios, choices, conversations, reasoning tasks, preferences, and behavioural questions.

The objective is breadth rather than certainty.

Phase-III - Pattern Investigation:
When repeated signals begin suggesting particular traits, interests, strenghts, weaknesses, fears, habits, preferences, or abilities, the interview investigates those patterns more deeply.

Phase-IV - Contradiction Testing:
Important conclusions are challenged using different situations and question styles.

The system should actively search for evidence that could disprove its current hypotheses rather than only confirming them.

Phase-V - Career Hypothesis Testing:
When possible career directions begin emerging, the system investigates the traits and requirements most important to those careers.

The career itself does not need to be revealed to the user during testing.

Phase-VI - Evidence Gap Investigation:
The system identifies important Human Profile areas that remain unknown, weakly supported, contradictory, or uncertain and prioritises them.

Phase-VII - Initial Discovery Completion:
The initial interview may finish when sufficient evidence exists to create a useful Human Profile and preliminary career hypotheses.

Completion does not mean the Human Profile is permanently complete.

Future conversations, activities, projects, achievements, failures, changing preferences, and new evidence may update the profile.

The Human Discovery Engine therefore remains active throughout the user's relationship with the platform.

#### Question Categories

The Human Discovery Engine may use multiple question categories so that the user cannot easily predict which trait is being investigated.

1. Situational Scenarios:
Present realistic or hypothetical situations and observe the user's decisions, priorities, reasoning, and reactions

2. Forced Trade-Off Questions:
Require the user to choose between competing desirable or undesirable outcomes when no option is obviously perfect.

3. Behavioural History Questions:
Ask what the user actually did in previous real situations rather than asking what the user believes they would do.

4. Reaction Questions:
Present unexpected events, pressure, criticism, failure, success, uncertainty, conflict, or opportunity and investigate the user's reaction.

5. Reasoning and Problem-Solving Tasks:
Give small logical, analytical, strategic, creative, spatial, numerical, verbal, or practical problems where appropriate.

6. Preference-Inference Questions:
Present activities, environments, subjects, experiences, or choices without directly asking the user to name their interests, likes or dislikes.

7. Resource Allocation Questions:
Ask the user to distribute limited time, money, attention, people, or other resources between competing objectives.

8. Social and Interpersonal Scenarios:
Explore communication, teamwork, leadership, persuasion, empathy, negotiation, conflict handling, trust, and social behaviour.

9. Ethical and Value Conflicts:
Present situations involving competing principles, responsibilities, incentives, loyalties, risks, or consequences.

10. Curiosity and Exploration Prompts:
Observe which subjects, mysteries, problems, activities, or unexplored possibilities naturally attract further investigation from the user.

11. Creation and Imagination Tasks:
Ask the user to design, invent, improve, explain, write, visualise, or propose something to investigate creativity and related abilities.

12. Practical Decision Tasks:
Present everyday or professional problems requiring prioritization, planning, organisation, judgement, or execution.

13. Contradiction Probes:
Re-examine an earlier inference using a substantially different scenario or question style.

14. Counterfactual Questions:
Change an important condition in an earlier situation and observe whether and why the user's decision changes.

15. Open Conversation:
Allow natural discussion in which unexpected interests, concerns, habits, reasoning patterns, motivations, strengths, weaknesses, and preferences may emerge without a predefined question format.

Question categories are investigative tools, not direct trait measurements.

A question may provide evidence about several Human Profile dimensions, and the same trait should ideally be investigated through different question categories.
